Welcome, Laura 👋

This page is the map to everything in this gallery: what was built, in what order, why it changed along the way, and the pieces it's made of. The prototypes are fully interactive HTML builds of two areas of the Kiki home-swap app: the Booking Request Flow (a guest asking to stay) and the Matches Flow (what happens between a request landing and a confirmed stay). Every numbered version in the gallery is frozen and stays openable, so you can compare any two rounds side by side. The highest number is always the live one; right now that's 3.7.

Driving the demos

From 3.0 onwards each prototype is two live phones side by side: the guest on the left (Melissa by default), the host Ryan on the right. They share state, so the host acting on a request updates the guest's phone instantly. Three bits of demo chrome sit outside the phones:

  • Restart demo (top right) resets everything to the starting point.
  • The guest label above the left phone is a picker: tap it to play any of the six requesters (Melissa, Aisha, Tash & Jordan, Priya, Sara, Marco), each with their live status. Guests mid-journey resume exactly where they were.
  • When an offer gets revoked, the picker is also how you peek at that guest's "offer revoked" screen, since the demo follows the winning guest.

The canonical demo run: host opens his trip → sends offers → guest accepts (others get revoked) → both sign the agreement → guest pays deposit + rent → both press Confirm match → celebrations → the match screen with the stay timeline.

Two supporting pages live beside the prototypes: the flow map (every pathway a request can take, including the trip model and the 48-hour checklist) and the card options page (the explorations behind the current card designs, kept for reference).

The two flows at a glance

Booking Request Flow (rounds 1 and 2)

Round 1 rebuilt the shipped 4-step flow faithfully (1.0), then explored three directions: best-practice polish (1.1), a restructured 3-step flow (1.2), and a review-centric, Airbnb-style hub with editor sheets (1.3). The client chose 1.3, and round 2 (2.0–2.4) refined that direction through client feedback: window stepper, the Staying/Hosting Trips split, the amber pending state, calendar work and an accessibility pass. 2.4 was the last active booking-flow build; its ideas carried into round 3.

Matches Flow (round 3, the main body of work)

Everything after a request is sent: the host's inbox and consent model, the 48-hour reserved window (signing + payments), mutual confirmation, the match screen, declines and revocations, and (3.7) listing restrictions. The full state machine is drawn on the flow map; the short version: New → Offered → Reserved → Confirmed, with Declined (reopenable) and Revoked (automatic) as the side exits.

Version history (1.0 to 3.7)

1.0–1.3Round 1: the fork in the road

1.0 is the control (the live app, rebuilt). 1.1 polished it, 1.2 restructured it, 1.3 replaced the wizard with one "Review and request" hub where Change buttons open dates/guests editor sheets. The client picked 1.3; every later flow builds on its editor-sheet pattern.

2.0–2.4Round 2: refining the chosen direction

Client-feedback rounds on 1.3: a 3-page flow with a window stepper and host preview (2.0), trimmed review card and live Trips tabs (2.1), Nina's Flat with three date windows plus the Staying/Hosting split and an accessibility pass (2.2), the amber pending state and calendar legend from the product review (2.3), and 2.4 as the final working build.

3.0The dual-phone stage

Guest and host side by side, both live and independent, sharing one state. This is the foundation every matches-flow idea is demoed on.

3.1The match screen

The post-confirmation home: a stay timeline (agreement, payments, condition photos, move-in/out, deposit refund), host instructions, check-in details and contacts. Real photos arrive here.

3.2Client review: rules of the window

The 48-hour reservation timer, withdrawal rules (either side can back out and everything unwinds), group bookings (Tash & Jordan), and a check-in section on the match screen.

3.3UI refinement round

The booker card, documents and cheques drawn as paper objects, the intro message as a letter, and a long back-and-forth on the footer: countdown placement, a tappable timer with a "why is there a countdown?" explainer, and the steps/CTA lockup.

WhyNick's screenshot-driven passes: aligning the two greens, keeping prices from jumping when labels change, replacing a script-font signature idea with a simple "Signed" label.
3.4The guided guest flow + mutual confirmation

Melissa's reserve steps became three horizontal screens (intro → sign → pay) instead of one checklist, and auto-confirmation was replaced by both sides explicitly pressing Confirm match. A signing "ceremony" was explored (flip the document over, sign with your finger).

Why"We want the signing of the document to feel a bit more significant." The draw-to-sign idea was built, then cut in 3.5 for a confirm dialog: ceremony kept, gimmick dropped.
3.5Full-screen agreement, groups, declines

The agreement opens as a full screen you scroll (X top right, buttons at the bottom), signing is a button plus a confirm dialog, group profiles appear, the first split-payments model lands, and the host gets a decline flow with reason categories. The left phone starts following whichever guest Ryan engages.

WhyColleague feedback: "this pop-up feels weird, you can still see the next button; maybe a full screen where you can scroll the terms." And three client questions: how do groups go through the flow, how do split payments work, what does a declined user look like to the host?
3.6The big client-review round (most of the current behaviour)

The longest round, driven by two client reviews and many walkthroughs with Nick:

  • Two-step consent: the host sends an offer; nothing is reserved until the guest accepts. Outs on both sides (withdraw offer, decline offer).
  • Multiple offers per trip: first guest to accept wins; other overlapping offers are revoked automatically, with both sides seeing honest "offer revoked" states. Declined requests can be reopened.
  • Split rent corrected: the trigger is stay duration (over 30 nights), not booking proximity. Deposit always upfront; a visible callout + toggle (pay in full / split monthly); month 1 inside the window, later months scheduled and payable from the match screen. Priya's 75-night stay demos three instalments.
  • Payments: a method sheet (bank transfer with copyable UK details and Copy all; Stripe as a flagged placeholder); one method decision covers all payments.
  • Profiles: full-screen swipeable profile cards with an Instagram screenshot (a client trust requirement) and position dots.
  • Statuses: the dot + coloured text system on both sides, design-system colours only.
  • Cards: host rows settled on H4b (two lines, right-aligned status column); guest cards on G1b (compact row, dates only, host's face on the photo corner).
  • Decline dialog reworked: the multi-choice reason goes to the guest, the free-text box goes only to the Kiki team and says so; reasons from the client's list; selections can be un-tapped.
  • Celebrations: "Your stay is confirmed!" / "Your match is confirmed!" with confetti; copy rules applied everywhere (no long dashes, the deposit-refund wording, the host-side "Dm us on instagram" farewell).
3.7Who-can-stay restrictions (current)

Listings can carry restrictions (currently placeholder categories: solo travellers only, women only, women preferred). They show as a chip on Explore cards, a tappable "Who can stay" section on the listing with plain-language explainer sheets, and inline warnings in the booking flow. Requests outside a host's preferences stay possible ("request anyway"), they're just clearly flagged as unlikely to be accepted.

WhyUsers were frustrated to discover restrictions only after requesting. The client's category list is still to come; the categories live in one map (RESTRICTION_INFO in src/data.ts) so swapping in the real list is a one-file change.

Decisions that shaped the design

Statuses are words, not badges

"I don't love these badge designs. I preferred the • with the text."Nick, reviewing pill-badge options
"These colours are crazy. Two greens? Grey for decline? Can you look at the design system colours and try again."Nick, on an early status palette

Result: one teal, one amber, red for closed states, grey only for revoked; always a 7px dot + bold text, never a pill. The full taxonomy is in the components section below.

Use the product's actual colours

"That green is not a green I've seen in the product."Nick, on action-hint colours

Result: all primary buttons use the lighter brand teal (--primary), action hints too; the darker teal (--primary-strong) is reserved for text that needs contrast. Tokens were ported 1:1 from the real app.

Features must announce themselves

"There's no way of me knowing that I had to click that."Nick, on a hidden split-payments link

Result: the split option became a visible callout with an explicit toggle. Same principle later drove the tappable countdown explainer and the restriction chips: anything with rules behind it gets a tap-to-learn-more.

Consent is symmetrical

The client's core model: hosts offer, guests accept, and neither side can be locked in. Every state has an exit (withdraw offer, decline offer, withdraw reservation, reopen a decline), and automatic transitions (revocation) are explained to both sides in plain words.

Who sees what, said out loud

The decline dialog separates the reason (sent to the guest) from the note (Kiki only) and labels both. The restriction explainers say requests stay visible to hosts. The reopen button admits it can't unsend the decline message. When in doubt, the UI states its audience.

Copy rules

  • No long dashes anywhere in product copy (periods, commas, colons or plain hyphens instead).
  • Deposit wording: "The deposit will be refunded in full after your stay assuming no damages/problems."
  • Celebrations: guest "Your stay is confirmed!", host "Your match is confirmed!".
  • Host-side match screen signs off "Dm us on instagram if you need anything"; guests get "Have a great stay!".

Known gaps and open threads

  • Restriction categories are placeholders. The client owes the real list; it drops into RESTRICTION_INFO in src/data.ts. Also flagged to the client: "Looking for a female" as a decline reason goes to the guest verbatim, worth a policy/legal look before production.
  • Payments are design-only. Stripe is a labelled placeholder; bank transfer is the real flow. The client scoped payments out of this sprint.
  • The guest's declined view is thin: a declined guest sees the status and a listing banner, but not the host's chosen reason category yet.
  • Known data quirk: Tash & Jordan's 31-night request sits under a trip header that reads "26 - 29 Aug · 3 nights" (flagged, accepted for the demo).
  • Instagram mocks reuse Ryan's apartment photos as the grid images.
  • Drag-to-reorder ranking (prioritising your sent requests) exists but is unreachable from the current UI, kept in the codebase in case prioritisation returns.
  • Profile faces are StyleGAN-generated (not real people), downscaled and embedded; Nina has no face asset yet (initial circle).

Recurring patterns without a picture
  • Explainer sheets: any rule gets a tap-to-learn-more dialog (countdown, restrictions): title, plain-language body, one Got it button.
  • Full-screen agreement: scrollable terms, X top right, Sign agreement at the bottom, then a confirm dialog ("You're signing as...").
  • Profile cards: full-screen horizontal snap cards per group member, with an Instagram screenshot block and position dots.
  • Avatars: one clear face whatever the group size; a dark +N badge carries the rest ("lead + count", chosen over pair collages after a five-guest stress test).
  • Celebrations: hero (photo deck or avatars), one-line title, one-shot confetti in brand-ish colours.
  • Decline dialog: radio reasons (sent to the guest, un-tappable to clear) + a free-text box labelled as Kiki-only.

Practical notes

Where things live. Repo: nhallam/Kiki-nick on GitHub. Each version is frozen on its own branch (matching-v30-base through matching-v37; round 1–2 on the booking-flow-* and round2* branches). The gallery launcher is built by gallery/build.mjs on the flow-gallery branch and served from main by Vercel at kiki-nick.vercel.app. The same gallery is mirrored to a private Claude artifact link for client sharing.

How a change ships. Work lands on the current version branch (today: matching-v37), the single-file build (dist/index.html) is committed with it, the gallery is rebuilt on flow-gallery and merged to main. New explorations get a new matching-v3x branch and a gallery tile, so old versions never change under the client.

The app itself is Vite + React + TypeScript, one self-contained HTML file per version. State for the whole two-phone demo lives in src/store.ts; most copy and data (listings, guests, restriction categories) in src/data.ts and src/screens/HostRequest.tsx (REQUEST_PREVIEWS).
